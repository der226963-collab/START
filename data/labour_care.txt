Labour and care time.
Labour is work that produces goods and services. Care is work that keeps people alive and well. Both are work, and both take time.
Paid work.
Those with jobs work 43 hours a week on average globally, according to a global dataset covering 160 countries and 97 percent of world population, published by IMF researchers Gethin and Saez.
The average adult worldwide, including those without employment, works about 25 hours each week in the same dataset. That dataset counts work that contributes to economic production, including unpaid agricultural work, and excludes unpaid home services like cleaning, cooking, and caring.
In middle-income countries such as India and Pakistan, working time for those with jobs exceeds 45 hours a week in the same dataset.
An earlier ILO report found average hours fell to 43.9 hours per week, while 35.4 percent of the world workforce worked more than 48 hours per week, and 20.3 percent worked less than 35 hours per week.
Long hours harm health, safety, and family life. Short hours can mean low income and insecure work. Both extremes are problems for planning.
Unpaid work.
Unpaid domestic and care work is work without pay in households and communities: cooking, cleaning, childcare, eldercare, and nursing sick family members.
Every day, 16.4 billion hours are dedicated to unpaid care work, according to ILO. That is comparable to 2 billion people working 8 hour shifts without compensation.
Women perform 76.2 percent of all unpaid care work, spending on average 3.2 times more time on these tasks than men.
Unpaid care work is valued at about 9 percent of global GDP, roughly 11 trillion US dollars, according to ILO.
In 2023, an estimated 708 million women aged 15 and over were outside the labour force primarily due to unpaid care responsibilities, compared with 40 million men.
Women and girls also spend an estimated 250 million hours every day collecting water, according to UN reports. That is unpaid work that is often not counted as care, but it is essential.
Time, planning, and fairness.
A plan has a time budget as well as a money budget and a carbon budget. Every plan needs hours of paid work, unpaid work, and rest.
Minimise necessary labour time, as PCAC states, but do not hide labour by moving it to unpaid care, to women, or to another country.
Share labour fairly. Share care fairly. Leave time for rest, learning, family, and community.
Count unpaid work in planning. If a plan assumes free care, free water collection, or free cooking, it is not a complete plan.
A shorter working week with no pay cut is a planning choice, not a technical impossibility. It needs more workers, better tools, or less output, and that tradeoff must be shown honestly.
People decide time priorities. Workers, carers, and communities must decide how time is shared, not a model alone.

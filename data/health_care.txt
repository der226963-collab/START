Health and care.
Health is physical, mental, and social wellbeing, not only absence of disease.
Care is work that keeps people alive and well: childcare, eldercare, nursing, cooking, cleaning, and emotional support.
Health and climate.
Extreme heat is the deadliest extreme weather for health. Between 2000 and 2019, about 489,000 heat-related deaths occurred each year, according to WMO estimates, with 45 percent in Asia and 36 percent in Europe.
Heat deaths are under-reported, because heat is often not listed as a cause of death. The true number may be higher.
Most at risk from heat are older adults, young children, pregnant women, outdoor workers, and people unhoused or living with chronic illness.
Heat health warning systems can save lives. Scaling them to 57 countries could save an estimated 98,314 lives per year, according to WHO and WMO analysis.
Between 2030 and 2050, climate change is expected to cause about 250,000 additional deaths per year from undernutrition, malaria, diarrhoea, and heat stress alone, according to WHO estimates cited by UN.
Air pollution harms lungs, heart, and brain. Wildfire smoke, traffic exhaust, and indoor smoke all harm health.
Water and sanitation protect health. Clean water, safe toilets, and handwashing prevent diarrhoea and other diseases.
Care work.
Care work is paid and unpaid. Paid care employs 381 million people globally, according to ILO care data.
Unpaid care work is larger and mostly invisible in GDP. Every day, 16.4 billion hours are dedicated to unpaid care work, according to ILO, comparable to 2 billion people working 8 hour shifts without pay.
Women perform 76.2 percent of all unpaid care work, spending on average 3.2 times more time on these tasks than men, according to ILO 2018 data.
Unpaid care work is valued at about 9 percent of global GDP, roughly 11 trillion US dollars, according to ILO.
In 2023, an estimated 708 million women aged 15 and over were outside the labour force primarily due to unpaid care responsibilities, compared with 40 million men.
By 2030, more than 2.3 billion people will need care, according to ILO, as populations age.
Care, labour, and planning.
A plan that ignores care will fail. Food, housing, and energy plans need time for cooking, cleaning, childcare, and eldercare.
Share care fairly. A plan must reduce, redistribute, and recognise care work, not assume women will do it for free.
Health care, childcare, and eldercare must be available, affordable, and close to home. Distance and cost are barriers to care.
Early warning, cooling centres, clean water, and health services are climate adaptation. They protect health before a crisis, not only after.
People decide health priorities. Health workers, patients, and carers must be part of planning, not only data points in a model.

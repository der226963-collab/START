Housing.
Adequate housing is more than a roof. The UN framework lists seven dimensions: security of tenure, availability of services, affordability, habitability, accessibility, location, and cultural adequacy.
Up to 3.4 billion people lack secure, safe, and adequate housing, according to UN-Habitat World Cities Report 2026. That is about 42 percent of humanity.
More than 1.1 billion people live in informal settlements and slums. In 2024, about 1.16 billion urban residents, almost one in four, lived in slums or informal settlements.
The global housing deficit increased from 251 million units in 2010 to 288 million units in 2023, according to UN-Habitat.
About 133 million people were displaced globally in 2024 due to conflict, violence, persecution, and disasters.
Buildings and energy.
Buildings and construction consume 32 percent of global energy and contribute 34 percent of global carbon dioxide emissions, according to UNEP Global Status Report for Buildings and Construction 2024-2025.
Operational emissions from buildings alone reached a record 9.8 gigatonnes in 2023 in the same report.
Materials like cement and steel are responsible for 18 percent of global emissions in that report, and are a major source of construction waste.
Building energy intensity fell by almost 10 percent between 2015 and 2023, while renewable energy share in final energy demand rose by nearly 5 percent.
Only 6 percent of buildings final energy consumption came from renewables in 2023, far below the 19 percent target for 2030 in UNEP reporting.
Housing, health, and climate.
A cold, damp, or overheated home harms health. Mould, cold, and heat increase illness, especially for children, older people, and people with chronic illness.
A home must protect from heat, flood, wind, and cold. Insulation, ventilation, shading, and drainage are health measures, not only comfort.
Retrofit improves existing homes without displacing residents. Insulation, windows, heating, and cooling upgrades reduce energy use and improve health.
Retrofit without eviction is a planning rule. Improving a home must not push its residents out.
Housing, location, and transport.
Location matters. A home far from work, school, health care, and food needs more transport, time, and money.
Dense, mixed neighbourhoods with walking, cycling, and public transport reduce transport emissions and time.
A housing plan must include water, sanitation, electricity, waste, and transport. A house without services is not adequate housing.
Housing and decisions.
People who live in a neighbourhood must decide its future. Regeneration must improve lives of existing residents, not replace them.
Count housing need in people and homes, not only in units built. A plan that builds homes no one can afford does not meet need.
Build, retrofit, and protect together. New homes, better existing homes, and secure tenure together meet housing need.

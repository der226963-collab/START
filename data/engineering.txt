Engineering.
Engineering turns science into systems that work in the real world.
A system has inputs, processes, outputs, and limits. Design starts with limits.
Energy engineering.
Electricity is generated, transmitted, stored, and used. Generation must meet demand at every moment.
Solar power converts light to electricity. Wind power converts moving air to electricity.
Hydropower converts falling water to electricity. Nuclear power converts heat from fission to electricity.
Coal and gas burn fuel to make heat, then electricity. Burning fuel releases carbon dioxide.
Renewables provided just over 30 percent of world electricity in 2023.
Renewables provided 14.6 percent of primary energy in 2023. Electricity and primary energy are different totals.
In 2023, about 510 gigawatts of renewable capacity were added worldwide. Solar was about three quarters of additions.
Storage stores energy for later use. Batteries store electricity. Pumped water stores potential energy.
A grid balances supply and demand. Storage, transmission, and flexible demand help balance a grid.
Efficiency means more service from less energy. Insulation reduces heating need. Efficient motors reduce electricity use.
Materials engineering.
Steel is made from iron ore, coal, and scrap. World steel production was about 1892 million tonnes in 2023.
Steel production released about 2800 million tonnes of carbon dioxide in 2023, about 8 percent of global greenhouse gases.
Scrap-based electric arc steel releases less carbon dioxide than blast furnace steel, well under 1 tonne per tonne, against a global average near 1.5 to 1.9 tonnes per tonne.
Cement is made from limestone and heat. Heating limestone releases carbon dioxide from the rock itself, not only from fuel.
About 60 percent of cement emissions come from the rock. Changing fuel alone cannot remove those emissions.
World averages are about 530 kilograms of cement and 240 kilograms of steel per person per year.
Reducing demand, reusing materials, and using less clinker can reduce cement and steel emissions.
Buildings engineering.
A building uses energy for heating, cooling, lighting, and hot water. Insulation, airtightness, and heat pumps reduce use.
A heat pump moves heat instead of making heat from fuel. It uses electricity more efficiently than resistance heating.
Retrofit means improving an existing building. Insulation, windows, and heating systems are retrofit measures.
Transport engineering.
Transport moves people and goods. Cars, buses, trains, ships, and planes use different amounts of energy per person or per tonne.
Electric trains and buses use electricity directly. Batteries store energy for electric vehicles.
Walking and cycling use human energy and need safe space, not fuel.
Water engineering.
Water is collected, treated, distributed, and returned. Pumps use energy. Leaks waste water and energy.
Rain, rivers, groundwater, and desalination are water sources. Each source has energy and environmental costs.
Systems engineering.
Design for maintenance. A system that cannot be maintained will fail.
Design for repair. Parts that can be replaced last longer than parts that cannot.
Design for safety. Test under failure, not only under normal conditions.
Measure performance. Compare actual output with predicted output. Learn from the difference.
A good engineering plan states assumptions, shows calculations, and can be checked by others.
Engineering, planning, and decisions.
Engineering calculates what is possible. Planning tests what meets needs within limits.
People decide which plan to use. Engineers do not decide for a community. They provide evidence for a decision.
Start with a simple system that works. Test it. Improve it. Scale what works.

The river runs low in August, and the farms, the town, and the fish all need water from the same shrinking stream. That is why I get up at four in the morning to fill my buckets. At six, when we are all ready, I start for the dam with a bundle of bricks and piles of wood that will keep us warm until dark. We stop every night on the way home or at some farmhouse near by to eat cheese, bread, and milk while I light more fires. Then off we go again, carrying our load as far as meagre water allows. Sometimes we run out before reaching the sea; sometimes not enough water reaches the ocean till it has filled the garbage heaps which lie just under its surface. But never do we want this giant body of clear blue H2O so much as I do."

Phosphorus is a limit that cannot be wished away, because the rivers carry whatever the fields cannot hold. A land of plenty is not possible in a world with such limits; and for that reason it must be planned differently. For one thing, arable farming should tend less strongly to into the countryside than outwards from it: the more narrow channels are built up as forests instead of ploughed over—and so on ad infinitum till there’s no left-over farmland at all but only forest or marshland (which would have been too good to waste). This pattern can hardly stand if food ends by sea rather than by air; still fewer people could live inland along any coast line than currently outside the Mediterranean rim—with much pityable armistice between city and hill and plain.

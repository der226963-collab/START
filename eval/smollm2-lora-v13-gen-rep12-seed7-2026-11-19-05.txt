The soil in the lower field is tired after years of the same crop, and the farmers are talking about what to plant instead. They say that they should grow wheat, oats and rye in the upper field. But only a few farmers have land for grain production; most of them are growing food for local people or animals rather than exporting it."

The Pimas' traditional agriculture has been put to ever greater test by population growth, rapid climate change and drought. "We're always moving," Alva says. "This is part of our life cycle as far-flung mountain dwellers who know how fragile everything can be when you multiply all your ties into one narrow line."

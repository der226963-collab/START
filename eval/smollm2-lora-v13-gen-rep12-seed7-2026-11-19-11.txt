If a machine drafts the words and a person decides what they mean, then the machine is a tool and the person is still in charge. If a machine ‘bakes’ what it produces, and a person decides how much of each ingredient to include or whether the food is sweet enough, then again the machine is still in charge. If a robot assembles something that needs human assembly, if only for safety reasons—and a separate person determines who gets to assemble which part—then once more we have machines doing work for us. And all these cases are different from one another: they differ by scale (how many people versus few devices), by degree (‘whether I decide or you do’) and also by intent (‘what goods should I make, for whom, and at whose expense?’)

Science.
Science starts with observation. We watch what happens and we measure it.
A question follows observation. What causes this change? What will happen next?
A hypothesis is a testable idea. It must be possible to prove it wrong.
We test a hypothesis with an experiment or with data. We record the result.
Evidence is data that supports or weakens an idea. One result is not proof.
We repeat a test to check the result. Repeatable results are stronger.
Measurement has units. Length in metres, mass in kilograms, time in seconds.
Energy in joules, power in watts, temperature in degrees Celsius.
Uncertainty is part of measurement. No measurement is perfectly exact.
We report a value and its uncertainty. A range is more honest than a single number.
Peer review means other scientists check the work. Checking finds errors.
Science changes when new evidence arrives. Old ideas are replaced by better ideas.
Mathematics.
Mathematics is the language of patterns and quantities.
Addition combines numbers. 2 plus 3 equals 5. 10 plus 20 equals 30.
Subtraction finds a difference. 10 minus 4 equals 6. 100 minus 25 equals 75.
Multiplication is repeated addition. 4 times 3 equals 12. 7 times 8 equals 56.
Division shares a number. 12 divided by 3 equals 4. 100 divided by 5 equals 20.
A variable stands for an unknown number. Let x equal 5. Then x plus 2 equals 7.
An equation states that two sides are equal. Solve for x in x plus 3 equals 10. Then x equals 7.
A function maps an input to an output. If f of x equals 2 times x, then f of 3 equals 6.
A graph shows how one quantity changes with another. Slope is rise divided by run.
Percent means per hundred. 50 percent of 200 equals 100. 10 percent of 80 equals 8.
An average summarises many numbers. Add the numbers and divide by how many there are.
The average of 4, 6, and 8 is 6, because 4 plus 6 plus 8 equals 18, and 18 divided by 3 equals 6.
Probability measures chance. A fair coin has probability 0.5 for heads and 0.5 for tails.
Optimisation finds the best value under limits. Minimise cost subject to a limit.
Maximise food subject to land less than 100 and water less than 50.
A constraint is a hard limit. Land, water, energy, and carbon are constraints.
A linear equation has the form a times x plus b equals y. A system of equations can be solved together.
Statistics describes data. Mean, median, and range summarise a distribution.
A correlation is not a cause. Two things can rise together for a third reason.
Climate change.
Climate is long-term weather. Weather is short-term. Climate change is a long-term shift.
The greenhouse effect warms the Earth. Greenhouse gases trap heat in the atmosphere.
Carbon dioxide is a greenhouse gas. Methane is a greenhouse gas. Nitrous oxide is a greenhouse gas.
Burning fossil fuels releases carbon dioxide. Coal, oil, and gas are fossil fuels.
Forests store carbon. Cutting forests releases carbon and reduces storage.
Oceans absorb heat and carbon dioxide. Warm oceans expand and sea level rises.
Melting ice on land adds water to oceans. Sea level rises when ice melts and water warms.
A carbon budget is the total carbon that can be emitted within a temperature limit.
If emissions exceed the budget, temperature rises further. A budget is a hard limit, not a target to exceed.
Temperature rise changes rainfall. Some places become drier, some become wetter.
Extreme weather becomes more likely. Heatwaves, floods, and droughts increase in frequency.
Climate justice means those who emitted least often face the worst impacts first.
Frontline communities need protection and resources first, not last.
Mitigation reduces emissions. Renewable energy, efficiency, and forests are mitigation.
Adaptation prepares for change. Flood defences, heat plans, and water storage are adaptation.
We need both mitigation and adaptation. Reducing emissions does not remove past change.
Planetary boundaries are limits for a safe Earth. Climate, land, water, and biodiversity are boundaries.
Crossing a boundary increases risk. Staying within boundaries is safer for everyone.
Predictive modelling.
A model is a simplified representation of a real system. It is not the system itself.
A model takes inputs and produces outputs. Inputs are data. Outputs are predictions.
Parameters are numbers inside a model. They are set from data or from theory.
A forecast predicts the future. A scenario explores what if a choice changes.
Validation tests a model against past data. If it cannot explain the past, do not trust it for the future.
A model has uncertainty. Inputs are uncertain, parameters are uncertain, and the future is uncertain.
Report a range, not a single number. A range shows what is known and what is not known.
A weather model uses temperature, pressure, wind, and humidity to forecast rain.
An energy model uses demand, supply, and storage to forecast power needs.
A crop model uses rain, temperature, and soil to forecast yield.
A food model uses population, diet, land, and water to forecast food need.
A planning model uses needs, resources, and limits to test a plan.
Test a plan against limits. If demand exceeds supply, the plan must change.
If a plan breaks a carbon limit, the plan must change. If a plan breaks a water limit, the plan must change.
Models do not decide. People decide, using model outputs as evidence.
A language model is not a predictive model. It predicts text, not tonnes, joules, or degrees.
Keep layers separate. Language helps explain a plan. Calculation checks a plan. People decide a plan.
A good model is transparent. Show inputs, show assumptions, show uncertainty, show limits.
A good model can be checked by others. If no one can check it, do not trust it for important decisions.
Science, mathematics, climate, and modelling together.
Science provides evidence. Mathematics provides calculation. Climate provides limits.
Modelling provides scenarios. Together they help people plan within planetary limits.
Meet needs for everyone. Stay within land, water, energy, and carbon limits.
Share labour fairly. Share care fairly. Leave time for rest and learning.
Start small, test, learn, and scale what works. Stop what does not work.

More science.
Energy cannot be created or destroyed. It changes form. Heat, light, and motion are forms of energy.
Power is energy per unit time. 100 watts for 10 seconds equals 1000 joules.
Water cycles through evaporation, condensation, and precipitation.
Photosynthesis uses light, water, and carbon dioxide to make plant matter and oxygen.
Respiration releases energy from food and produces carbon dioxide and water.
Soil holds water and nutrients. Healthy soil supports crops and stores carbon.
More mathematics.
If demand is 1170 tonnes and supply is 1049 tonnes, then shortage equals 121 tonnes.
Shortage equals demand minus supply. Supply equals demand minus shortage.
If 200 people each need 2 kilograms of food per day, then total need equals 400 kilograms per day.
If land is 100 hectares and each hectare yields 5 tonnes, then total yield equals 500 tonnes.
A rate is change divided by time. If temperature rises 1 degree in 50 years, the rate is 0.02 degrees per year.
A ratio compares two numbers. A ratio of 2 to 1 means the first is twice the second.
More climate.
Renewable energy comes from sun, wind, water, and heat from the Earth.
Solar power converts light to electricity. Wind power converts moving air to electricity.
Storage stores energy for later use. Batteries and pumped water are forms of storage.
Efficiency means getting more service from less energy. Insulation reduces heating need.
Transport, buildings, food, and industry all use energy and produce emissions.
Reducing demand and changing supply both reduce emissions.
More predictive modelling.
Choose a question first. What do we need to forecast, and for whom?
Choose data next. Check source, date, quality, and permission.
Build a simple model first. A simple model that can be checked beats a complex model that cannot.
Test the model on data it has not seen. This is called validation.
If the model fails validation, revise the model or collect better data.
Compare scenarios. Scenario A: current trend. Scenario B: lower emissions. Scenario C: local supply.
Report differences between scenarios. Report uncertainty for each scenario.
Do not hide uncertainty. Do not present a forecast as a fact.
A forecast is a range of possible futures, given assumptions.
Change the assumption, and the forecast changes. Show that change.
People set goals and limits. Models test whether a plan meets goals within limits.
Assemblies decide. Models assist. Language models explain. Calculations check.
